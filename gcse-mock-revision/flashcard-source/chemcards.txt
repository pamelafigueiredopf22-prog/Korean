Key concepts|Relative charge and mass of a proton, neutron, electron?|Proton +1, mass 1. Neutron 0, mass 1. Electron −1, mass tiny (about 1/1836).
Key concepts|Where are protons, neutrons and electrons?|Protons and neutrons in the nucleus; electrons in shells around it.
Key concepts|What does the atomic number tell you?|The number of protons (= number of electrons in an atom).
Key concepts|How do you work out the number of neutrons?|Mass number − atomic number.
Key concepts|Sodium: mass number 23, atomic number 11. Protons, neutrons, electrons?|11 protons, 12 neutrons, 11 electrons.
Key concepts|What are isotopes?|Atoms of the same element (same protons) with different numbers of neutrons.
Key concepts|Electron arrangement of sodium (11) and chlorine (17)?|Sodium 2.8.1. Chlorine 2.8.7.
Key concepts|What does the group number tell you? The period number?|Group = number of outer electrons. Period = number of shells.
Key concepts|What did Mendeleev do?|Arranged elements in a periodic table, left gaps for undiscovered elements and predicted their properties.
Key concepts|How do metals and non-metals form ions?|Metals lose electrons → positive ions. Non-metals gain electrons → negative ions.
Key concepts|What is ionic bonding?|Metal + non-metal: electrons transferred; oppositely charged ions held by strong electrostatic attraction.
Key concepts|When do ionic compounds conduct electricity?|When molten or dissolved (ions free to move).
Key concepts|What is covalent bonding?|Non-metal atoms sharing pairs of electrons.
Key concepts|Why do simple molecular substances have low melting points?|Weak forces between the molecules are easy to overcome.

Key concepts|Formula of magnesium chloride (Mg²⁺, Cl⁻)?|MgCl₂ (charges must cancel).

Key concepts|A reaction seems to lose mass. Why?|A gas was produced and escaped.

Rates|What is the rate of reaction?|How fast reactants are used up or products are formed.
Rates|60 cm³ of gas in 30 s. Mean rate?|60 ÷ 30 = 2 cm³/s
Rates|What is activation energy?|The minimum energy particles need to react when they collide.
Rates|Why does increasing temperature increase rate?|Particles move faster → more frequent collisions AND more collisions with enough energy.
Rates|Why does increasing concentration increase rate?|More particles in the same volume → more frequent collisions.
Rates|Why does increasing gas pressure increase rate?|Same particles in a smaller space → more frequent collisions.
Rates|Why does powder react faster than lumps?|Bigger surface area → more particles exposed → more frequent collisions.
Rates|What is a catalyst? What are enzymes?|Speeds up a reaction without being used up, by lowering activation energy. Enzymes = biological catalysts.
Rates|Core practical: marble chips + hydrochloric acid. What do you measure?|Volume of CO₂ gas (gas syringe) or mass lost (balance) over time.
Rates|Core practical: sodium thiosulfate + hydrochloric acid. What do you measure?|Time for the cross to disappear (the solution goes cloudy).
Rates|What does a flat line on a rate graph mean?|The reaction has finished (a reactant has run out).
Energy|Exothermic vs endothermic?|Exothermic gives out heat (temperature rises). Endothermic takes in heat (temperature falls).
Energy|Two examples of exothermic reactions?|Combustion and neutralisation.
Energy|Two examples of endothermic reactions?|Thermal decomposition; dissolving ammonium nitrate (also citric acid + sodium hydrogencarbonate).
Energy|Why use a polystyrene cup in energy practicals?|It is an insulator, so less heat is lost.
Energy|Is bond breaking exothermic or endothermic? Bond making?|Breaking: endothermic (takes energy). Making: exothermic (releases energy).
Energy|Formula for energy change using bond energies?|Energy to break bonds − energy released making bonds. Negative = exothermic.
Energy|H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. H–H 436, Cl–Cl 242, H–Cl 431. Energy change?|678 − 862 = −184 kJ/mol (exothermic)
Energy|On a reaction profile, how do you spot an exothermic reaction?|Products are lower in energy than reactants.
Energy|On a reaction profile, what is the activation energy?|The rise from the reactants to the top of the curve.

Fuels|Uses: petrol, kerosene, diesel oil, fuel oil, bitumen?|Cars; aircraft; cars and trains; ships and power stations; roads and roofs.
